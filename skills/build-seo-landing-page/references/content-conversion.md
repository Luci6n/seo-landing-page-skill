## Core Principle

Write for the visitor's decision, not only for search engines.

A good landing page should quickly answer:

- What is this?
- Is it for me?
- Where does it serve?
- Why should I trust it?
- What should I do next?

## Page Copy Structure

For most landing pages, use this order:

1. Identity: brand/entity, category, location or audience.
2. Main value: what problem is solved or what outcome is offered.
3. Primary CTA: contact, book, buy, quote, WhatsApp, email, or call.
4. Service/offer details: concrete list of what is included.
5. Trust: real photos, reviews, credentials, years, portfolio, clients, guarantees, or clear policies.
6. Practical facts: hours, address, delivery/service areas, pricing notes, availability.
7. FAQ: answer real objections and contact-before-action questions.
8. Footer: repeat contact and entity facts.

## CTA Guidance

Use one primary conversion action per page when possible.

Good CTA labels are specific:

- WhatsApp now
- Call for a quote
- Book an appointment
- Get directions
- Email the team
- View menu
- Start free trial

Avoid vague CTA labels when the next action is obvious:

- Learn more
- Click here
- Submit
- Explore

Secondary CTAs should support the same decision, such as map directions, copy address, brochure download, or email.

## Trust Signals

Use only real trust signals.

Good trust signals:

- Real customer reviews from legitimate platforms.
- Real shop, team, product, workplace, or project photos.
- Business registration, license, certification, or trade association where relevant.
- Real service areas, opening hours, and contact details.
- Clear pricing notes when exact pricing is not possible.
- Portfolio, case studies, or completed work examples.

Avoid:

- Fake ratings.
- Fake reviews.
- Unsupported "best", "number one", or "guaranteed" claims.
- Stock-like visuals when users need to inspect a real place, product, or person.

## Local Business Copy

For local businesses, mention:

- Exact business name.
- Primary service category.
- City, neighborhood, state, or service area.
- Contact method.
- Opening hours or appointment expectations.
- Whether customers should call/WhatsApp before visiting.

Use natural phrasing. Do not repeat locations unnaturally just for keywords.

## SaaS Or Product Copy

For SaaS/product pages, mention:

- User role or audience.
- Main workflow or pain point.
- Key differentiator.
- Proof such as customers, integrations, security posture, or case studies.
- Pricing or trial path when available.

Avoid making the hero headline a vague slogan if the product is not already famous.

## Portfolio Or Personal Site Copy

For portfolio pages, mention:

- Name or professional identity.
- What the person builds or offers.
- Strongest proof: projects, writing, clients, GitHub, demos, Kaggle, research, or talks.
- Clear contact path.

Keep links labeled clearly and make external profiles easy to scan.

## Multilingual Copy

When supporting multiple languages:

- Keep business facts consistent across languages.
- Translate CTAs and service descriptions, not only navigation.
- Keep phone, address, hours, and service areas identical unless the business truly changes by market.
- Avoid machine-like literal translations for trust-critical copy.

## AEO/GEO Copy

Answer-engine and generative-engine readiness improves when copy is:

- Factual.
- Specific.
- Entity-rich.
- Easy to quote or summarize.
- Consistent with structured data.

Lead with the answer: state what the business does, for whom, and where in the opening lines, before supporting detail. A short self-contained opening paragraph is easier for a person to skim and easier for an assistant to quote. This is a writing convention, not a documented ranking rule — do not present a specific word count to a client as a requirement.

Good FAQ answers are short but complete. Put important facts in visible HTML, not only metadata.

Keep genuinely time-sensitive facts (hours, pricing notes, service list) current, and show a visible last-updated date when the page or its facts change over time. AI systems weigh recency when choosing which sources to cite, but only claim a date that reflects a real edit.
